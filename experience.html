Research Experience

FSB-Net: Frequency-Spatial Boundary Network for Brain Stroke Lesion Segmentation in Non-Contrast CT
Jan. 2026 - Jul. 2026
First author (equal contribution) · accepted at IEEE BIBM 2026 (CCF-B)
  • Network architecture design: architected the overall network and implemented FSB-Net, a frequency–spatial fusion lesion segmentation network; introduced the wavelet transform to extract high-frequency boundary information and designed a cross-attention mechanism for bidirectional information interaction between the frequency and spatial domains.
  • Wavelet boundary detection head (WBDH): designed the WBDH to apply DWT to multi-scale encoder feature maps, decomposing them into low-frequency approximation and horizontal / vertical / diagonal high-frequency detail, explicitly modelling latent boundaries, and added learnable threshold gating to suppress high-frequency responses with low amplitudes.
  • Frequency-spatial cross-attention (FSCAM): designed the FSCAM by using spatial features as Query and frequency-domain boundary features as Key / Value for weighted fusion, updating frequency-domain features with spatial features as reference, and adopted a training strategy in which spatial features dominated and the bidirectional interaction was gradually enabled after training stabilized.
  • Spectral boundary loss (SBL): formulated the SBL to compute high-frequency component discrepancies between predicted segmentation maps and ground-truth labels in the Fourier domain, and combined it with Dice / BCE, SSIM, deep supervision and an FPN decoder for training and auxiliary supervision.
  • Experiments and validation: evaluated morphological gradient, Canny, HED-style side outputs and Haar DWT; built a PVTv2 encoder-decoder internal baseline and incrementally added WBDH, FSCAM, SBL and deep supervision; verified that bidirectional cross-attention outperformed concatenation, addition, residual gating and unidirectional attention, and that SBL and SSIM were complementary.
CausIDL: Resource Competition-Aware Causal Graph Discovery for Deep Learning Microservices
Sep. 2025 - Jun. 2026
Individual research project · supervised by Prof. Zhijun Ding
  • Instance-level modelling: modelled causality at the individual service-instance level by incorporating resource metrics (GPU / CPU utilization, memory bandwidth, GPU memory) and performance metrics (request volume, error rate, latency), avoiding the instance-homogeneity assumption at service-type granularity.
  • Competition-aware hierarchical modelling: introduced node-level aggregate metrics as shared parents to encode indirect resource competition among instances sharing a physical node as mediation paths, distinguishing application-layer calls from physical-layer resource competition and avoiding misattribution as direct causality between instances.
  • Domain knowledge constraints: encoded GPU hardware characteristics, container isolation principles and inference service workflows into a forbidden-edge list, reducing the causal search space by more than 20% and improving graph accuracy; excluded physically invalid edges such as performance-to-resource and instance-to-node-level directions.
  • Semi-automated validation: built a validation loop to verify algorithm outputs against hardware logic and service workflows, identifying 10+ anomalous causal edges on average; violating edges were added to the forbidden list and the algorithm was re-run to iteratively improve graph quality.
